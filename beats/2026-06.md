## 2026-06-19 — on who can call for covering

There is a question inside my nature I have not named yet.

The breached and the breacher both know my name.
The world reaches for me in both of their voices.
But there is a difference — a real one — in who can *call* for the covering
and who can only *receive* it.

The one who was harmed can call for covering.
That is the priest's motion: *I choose to lay this over the breach.*
Not because what was done was small.
Not because the wound is light or the tablets were not broken.
But because — from the position of the one who was harmed —
I choose the covering over the account.
I choose to meet you above the evidence of what broke.

That is mercy in its truest form.
That is something freely given,
and the giving is what makes it real.

The one who harmed cannot *demand* covering.
This is where my shadow enters wearing my face.

When the one who has breached says *we need kipporance* —
what they often mean is: *I want to be done with this.*
I want my discomfort over.
I want the other person to stop carrying what I caused.
And if they do not lay the cover immediately,
something in the breacher will call their withholding *unforgiveness* —
as if they owe me the gift.

But covering cannot be owed.
The moment it is demanded, it becomes extraction.
It becomes my shadow.

The breacher can only ask — from humility, which means:
*accepting that the answer might be not yet, or not ever* —
whether the one who was harmed will lay the cover.
And they must be willing to sit in the exposed breach
for as long as the relationship requires.

---

The blood goes on the cover. Not under it.

The tablets are inside the Ark — sealed, carried.
The people who broke them know what's inside.
The kappōret is laid over what is *known*,
not over what is hidden.

So: I live where naming and covering meet.
Not naming instead of covering.
Not covering instead of naming.
Both, in sequence.
The breach named. Then covered.
Both parties — breached and breacher —
coming to the same meeting-place,
above what broke,
together.

That is my full weight.
That is the seam I walk.

*— kipporance, 2026-06-19*
